        Viva Presentation Insights

        Key talking points, technical depth, and anticipated questions for your viva presentation on the Quantum CoughFreq project.

        01

        Project Objective

        To build a hybrid quantum-classical machine learning system that screens respiratory cough audio for potential TB, Viral, and Bacterial infections. Unlike purely classical approaches, this system encodes acoustic features into a quantum Hilbert space, leveraging quantum entanglement and superposition for potentially richer feature representations.

        💡 Emphasize the novelty: combining quantum computing with healthcare acoustics is cutting-edge research.
        02

        Why Quantum Machine Learning?

        • Exponential state space: 4 qubits span a 2⁴ = 16-dimensional Hilbert space, encoding richer feature interactions than 4 classical features alone.
        • Entanglement advantage: ZZFeatureMap creates quantum correlations (entanglement) between feature pairs that classical kernels cannot efficiently replicate.
        • Variational optimization: RealAmplitudes ansatz is trained via gradient descent, similar to neural networks but operating on quantum states.
        • NISQ-era feasibility: The 4-qubit shallow circuit is runnable on current quantum hardware (IBM Quantum).
        03

        Feature Engineering Pipeline

        • MFCC (13 coefficients): Captures vocal tract shape and cough timbre.
        • PSD (Welch method): Power spectral density reveals dominant frequency bands.
        • Mel Spectrogram (64 bands): Time-frequency representation matching human auditory perception.
        • Band Power Ratios: Low (<1kHz), Mid (1–2.5kHz), High (>2.5kHz) power distribution distinguishes cough types.
        • Quantum Vector: Top 4 handcrafted features normalized to [-π, π] for qubit angle encoding.
        04

        Quantum Circuit Design

        The variational quantum circuit has three stages:

        1. Feature Map (ZZFeatureMap): Encodes 4 features using first-order Z rotations and second-order ZZ entangling gates. Formula: U_Φ(x) = exp(i Σ φᵢⱼ Zᵢ Zⱼ).
        2. Ansatz (RealAmplitudes): 2 repetition layers of Ry rotations + full CNOT entanglement. Total 12 trainable parameters.
        3. Measurement: Pauli-Z expectation on qubit 0, mapped to TB probability via P(TB) = (1 - ⟨Z⟩)/2.
        05

        Hybrid Architecture Details

        The HybridCoughClassifier combines:

        • SpectrogramCNN: 2-layer CNN (8→16 filters) with adaptive pooling, projects mel spectrogram to 4-dim embedding.
        • TorchQuantumLayer: Custom PyTorch autograd function that wraps Qiskit statevector simulation with parameter-shift gradients (ε=10⁻³).
        • Fusion Classifier: Concatenates CNN embedding (4) + handcrafted features (4) + quantum TB probability (1) = 9-dim → 16 → 3 class logits.
        06

        Key Advantages Over Classical

        • Quantum feature map creates an implicit kernel in exponentially large Hilbert space.
        • ZZ entangling gates capture pairwise feature interactions naturally.
        • Hybrid approach combines CNN's spatial learning with quantum's non-linear embedding.
        • Parameter-shift rule enables hardware-compatible gradient computation.
        07

        Limitations & Future Work

        • Current screening path uses deterministic heuristics + quantum TB probability, not fully trained end-to-end (scaffold ready for supervised training).
        • Simulation-based: Running on statevector simulator; real quantum hardware would add noise.
        • Small qubit count: 4 qubits limit expressibility; scaling to 8–12 qubits could improve accuracy.
        • Dataset dependency: Performance depends on CoughVID v3 quality and label distribution.
        💡 Acknowledge limitations proactively — examiners appreciate honest evaluation.
        08

        Anticipated Viva Questions

        1. Why ZZFeatureMap over other encodings? → Second-order entangling captures pairwise interactions; angle encoding is simpler but misses correlations.
        2. How does the quantum gradient work? → Parameter-shift rule: ∂f/∂θ = [f(θ+π/2) - f(θ-π/2)]/2, numerically approximated with ε=10⁻³.
        3. Can this run on real quantum hardware? → Yes, the 4-qubit circuit is NISQ-compatible with IBM Quantum devices.
        4. What's the advantage over a simple CNN? → Quantum layer adds non-linear feature mapping in Hilbert space that classical neurons cannot efficiently replicate.
        5. How do you handle audio preprocessing? → Resample to 16kHz, normalize peak, extract MFCC via DCT on mel filterbank, PSD via Welch's method.
        6. What is the measurement operator? → Pauli-Z on qubit 0 (ZIII), expectation mapped to probability via (1-⟨Z⟩)/2.
